# The Big Bet: A Third Business With More Upside (3 Nov 2026)

**The brief:** you are now willing to put in more hours if the upside is materially bigger. That changes the answer. Buying distributor books (see `third-business-decision.md`) stays as part of the MFD lane. The **third business** should be a large, separate profit pool.

## The decision

**Mumbai housing-society redevelopment, entered from the finance side and scaled up to co-development.**

1. **Phase 1, months 0-18: society-side redevelopment adviser.** Little capital needed. You help old societies choose a developer, structure the deal and protect members' money (corpus, rent, bank guarantees, escrow). You work alongside a licensed architect/PMC firm.
2. **Phase 2, months 18-48: co-developer.** Take the smaller societies that big developers ignore, as a JV partner with an established contractor or mid-size developer. You bring the society, the feasibility work and the financing. They bring execution.
3. **Phase 3, year 4 onward: your own development platform.** Possibly add a regulated real-estate credit or equity vehicle (Cat II AIF) once you have a track record.

**Time:** 25-35 hours a week. This is the one option where extra hours turn into tens of crores, not lakhs.

---

## 1. Why redevelopment wins

### The profit pool is very large and concentrated where you are
- The redevelopment pipeline is estimated at **₹1.3-1.5 lakh crore**. Knight Frank estimates ~44,277 apartments worth ₹1.30 lakh Cr entering the market by 2030. Palladian puts the pipeline at ₹1.5 lakh Cr and ~59,000 new homes.
- **1,094 society redevelopment agreements** have been signed since 2020, about **70% of them in the western suburbs**.
- Mumbai has very little open land, so redevelopment is now the main way new supply gets built, and the main way outside developers enter the city.

### Your edge is real, and it is the scarce input
Societies have plenty of architects and builders to choose from. What they lack is **someone they trust to judge the money side**:
- Is this developer financially sound?
- Are the corpus and rent secured?
- What happens if the project stalls?

Stalled projects are a known fear in Mumbai. One industry blog claims thousands; I could not verify that number. As an MFD, you already:
- have the trust of Mumbai families, many of whom live in 30-50-year-old societies
- can read balance sheets, model cash flows and structure security such as bank guarantees and escrow
- understand how a society's general body makes decisions

**Winning the society's mandate is the bottleneck in this business, and that is exactly where you are strongest.**

### It feeds your MFD business directly
Redevelopment pays members a **corpus fund, rent during construction, and often hardship compensation**. Those are lump sums of lakhs to crores per family, and they need a home. Every society you advise is a source of AUM for your MFD and future PRIM business. (Keep the roles cleanly separated and disclosed. See Risks.)

### The rules are getting clearer
- Section 79A of the MCS Act requires the developer to be selected at a general-body meeting **in the presence of a registrar's official, with video recording**, and makes a PMC mandatory.
- A new Maharashtra Cooperation Department GR dated **30 Sep 2026** reportedly replaces the 2019 GR, with more emphasis on written consent and competitive developer selection. I saw this in a secondary source; check the official text.
- More process and competitive bidding favour a credible, process-driven adviser over a local fixer.
- DCPR 2034 **33(7)(B)** gives incentive FSI (the higher of 10 sq m per member or 15% of authorised built-up area), and the free-sale flats this creates are where the developer's profit comes from.

## 2. Illustrative economics (my assumptions, not market data)

I found **no published margin or fee data** for 33(7)(B) projects or PMC/advisory mandates. Validate the numbers below with 3 PMC firms and 2 mid-size developers in your first 30 days.

**Phase 1: advisory**

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Fee per society mandate (finance and developer selection) | ₹15-50 L, depending on size, plus a success fee on signing |
| Mandates per year once established | 6-10 |
| Annual revenue | ₹1-4 Cr, with little capital needed |

**Phase 2: co-development (a typical small society that big developers skip)**

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Sale component (free-sale flats) | ₹80-200 Cr |
| Developer profit before tax | 15-20% of sales ≈ ₹12-40 Cr over 4-5 years |
| Your JV share (society, feasibility and financing; partner executes) | 30-40% ≈ **₹4-15 Cr per project** |
| Your capital per project | ₹3-10 Cr (approvals, rent/corpus guarantees, early costs). Construction is largely funded by sales and construction finance. |

**Year 5 target:** 3-4 projects running at the same time, plus the advisory pipeline. That could mean **₹10-25 Cr a year** in profit, which is an order of magnitude above any other option on this list.

## 4. Ninety-day plan

**Days 1-30: validate the economics**
- Meet 3 PMC/architect firms and 2 mid-size redevelopment developers. Get real numbers on fees, margins, timelines and what goes wrong.
- Read the 30 Sep 2026 GR, Section 79A and DCPR 33(7)(B), with a redevelopment lawyer.
- Survey your own client base: which clients live in societies 30+ years old, and which are already discussing redevelopment?

**Days 31-60: set up the practice**
- Form an LLP for the advisory work, separate from the MFD entity.
- Partner with one licensed PMC/architect firm. They do the technical PMC role; you lead feasibility, developer financial due diligence and the security structure.
- Build a standard **Developer Financial Health Scorecard** (net worth, past delivery, stalled projects, MahaRERA record, lenders, litigation) and a **Member Protection Term Sheet** (bank guarantee for rent and corpus, escrow, timelines, penalties). These two documents are your product.
- Content: a short series on "How to redevelop safely". It builds a local reputation quickly. Keep it separate from the Kings channel.

**Days 61-90: first mandates**
- Pitch to 15 societies, starting with your clients' societies. Aim for 2 signed advisory mandates.
- Open JV conversations with 2 developers or contractors to bid jointly for a small society in Phase 2.

**Stop rule.** If after 9 months you have fewer than 2 paid mandates, or the validated margins are below 12% of sales, return to the solar option.

## 5. Risks and safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Long project cycles (4-6 years), approval delays | Advisory income in Phase 1 pays the bills. Only go into JVs once the plan is approved and member consents are signed. |
| Stalled projects and reputational damage | Never compromise on bank guarantees for rent and corpus. Choose JV partners with a delivery track record. Protecting your reputation comes before any single deal. |
| **Conflict between your roles** (adviser to the society, developer, MFD to the members) | Never advise a society and develop the same project. Disclose your MFD role in writing. Members choose freely where to invest their corpus. |
| **Raising money from your MFD clients for projects** | Don't raise it informally. Pooled money from investors must go through a regulated vehicle (for example a SEBI AIF) with legal advice. Informal pooling can create deposit or collective-investment-scheme liability. |
| Politics and local power in redevelopment | Work through formal 79A and registrar processes with full documentation and video. Keep all dealings transparent. |
| Big developers competing in the western suburbs | Focus on smaller societies (20-60 flats) that are below the big players' minimum deal size. |
