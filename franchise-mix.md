# Adding Franchises to the Mix (24 Oct 2026)

**Brief:** ₹2 Cr ± 50% (₹1-3 Cr), Mumbai or Delhi NCR, you prefer distribution, held through a manager. Now add franchise options.
**Confidence warning:** this is the weakest-sourced file in the repo. Franchisors do not publish audited outlet-level profit, and almost every investment, payback or ROI figure below comes from franchise-marketing blogs or the franchisor's own claims. I could not open the official franchise pages for Amul, Apollo Pharmacy or Haldiram's (404), and two comparison sites returned 403. Treat every number here as a **question to put to the franchisor**, not a fact. The only hard store-level data I found is MedPlus's company-owned stores (below).
**Compliance:** kept light, as asked.

## 1. Franchise vs. buying a business, in plain terms

- **Franchise:** you pay a fee, build or lease a unit, follow the brand's rules, and typically pay royalty (a few % of sales, e.g. 4-8% quoted for QSR by one blog). Brand and supply systems are provided. You own an outlet, not a customer base, so there is no goodwill to buy and no seller to trust.
- **Resale franchise:** a few outlets are sold on SMERGERS, but they are small and need the franchisor's approval (see Section 4).
- **Not passive:** the sources agree franchising "is not passive income", and outlets with an involved owner outperform absentee-owned ones. A manager-run outlet is possible, but plan to lose a slice of profit to the manager and to visit often.

**Why A:** the businesses you can inspect are the ones with books to check. Use the franchise sleeve to test a model rather than to carry your returns.

## 5. Franchise due diligence (do this before paying any fee)

1. Ask for the **franchise agreement** and read royalty, marketing fund, **territory exclusivity**, term, renewal and resale or exit rights.
2. Get **outlet-level monthly P&Ls for at least three existing franchisees in the same city**, and call them without the franchisor present.
3. Ask how many **company-owned outlets** operate within 2-3 km, and how many franchisees closed or left in the last 3 years.
4. Insist on the **total cost in writing**: fee, fit-out, deposit, opening stock, software, marketing, and 6-12 months of working capital.
5. Check who must be present (a pharmacist, a manager approved by the brand) and what manager cost does to your profit. A ₹8-10 lakh manager can take a third of a small outlet's profit.
6. Pay only through the franchisor's official channel, against a signed agreement.
