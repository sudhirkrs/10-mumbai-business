# The Third Business: Decision Memo (3 Nov 2026)

## The decision

**Third business: buy the books of retiring mutual fund distributors.** Each purchase is a "book" deal: the retiring MFD transfers their AUM to your ARN, and you buy the trail income stream.

Don't start a third unrelated business. Run a capital-allocation business that sits on top of the MFD you already run. It is the only option I found that does all of the following:
- adds income without adding a new skill
- earns recurring income, not one-off deals
- moves you closer to the PMS licence

Your three lanes then become:

| Lane | Role | Time share |
|---|---|---|
| 1. MFD + book acquisitions | Cash engine. Grow AUM organically and by buying it. | ~60% |
| 2. PMS / PRIM | Upgrade path for ₹25L+ clients. Licence preparation starts now. | ~15% (rising later) |
| 3. History of Kings content | Brand and audience asset, produced in fixed batches. Not a cash engine in years 1-2. | ~15% |
| Admin / compliance | | ~10% |

This is one business with three faces. It is not three businesses.

---

## 1. How I chose: five filters

A third business is only worth your time if it passes all five:

1. **It uses an asset you already own:** client trust, ARN and operations, audience, or capital. No new customer-acquisition engine.
2. **It earns recurring income.** You get paid every month, not per deal.
3. **Regulation is helping it, not hurting it.**
4. **It runs on ≤10 hours a week once it is set up.**
5. **It moves the PMS plan forward,** through net worth, AUM or track record.

| Candidate | 1 Asset | 2 Recurring | 3 Regulation | 4 Time | 5 Feeds PMS | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Buying MFD books** | ✅ ARN, ops, capital | ✅ Trail income | ✅ AMFI explicitly allows transfer on voluntary exit | ✅ Deal-driven | ✅ AUM and net worth | **Pick** |
| Insurance distribution | ✅ Same clients | ❌ IRDAI proposes cutting renewal commission | ❌ See below | ✅ | ⚠️ | Reject as a business. Keep only as a client service. |
| Buying an SME (CNC, chemicals, etc. from the Oct shortlist) | ⚠️ Capital only | ✅ | Neutral | ❌ Plant, staff, outside Mumbai | ⚠️ | Reject for now. Too distracting. |
| Mumbai real-estate channel partner | ✅ HNI clients | ❌ Lumpy | Neutral | ❌ Site visits, follow-ups | ❌ | Reject. Also a conflict with your advice role. |
| SEBI RIA / research analyst | ✅ Knowledge | ✅ | ❌ Must be segregated from distribution | ⚠️ | ⚠️ | Reject. Would split the same clients. |
| Heritage-travel tours (to monetise the Kings audience) | ✅ Audience | ❌ Seasonal | Neutral | ❌ Ops-heavy | ❌ | Reject. Revisit only once the channel is large. |

**Why insurance fails now.** IRDAI's September 2026 consultation, "Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution", proposes capping individual health insurance commissions. First-year commission would be about 15% for distribution firms and 20% for agents. Renewal commission would be **5% for firms and 10% for agents**. Possible start dates are 1 Jan or 1 Apr 2027. A renewal book built on that is not "serious money", so sell insurance only as a service to clients.

## 2. Why buying books works

### Sellers exist
- Only about **7.5%** of individual MFDs are meaningfully active (Cafemutual, using AMC data, March 2026). There are thousands of small books whose owners are ageing or have stopped working them. About 13,451 individual MFDs have AUM above ₹25 Cr.
- Consolidation has started. In November 2025, three Kolkata MFDs merged into Asset Kraft, with about ₹700 Cr AUM and 5,000 investors. Only 125 individual MFDs have crossed ₹1,000 Cr. Scale goes to whoever is buying.
- Crisil and ICICI Pru AMC project MFD-sourced AUM at **₹44.4 lakh Cr by FY29**, so the pool is growing.

### The rules allow it
- AMFI's transfer-of-AUM guidelines list **merger, acquisition, transfer of business and voluntary cessation** as valid reasons to move AUM to another ARN.
- The incoming distributor **does** receive trail when the outgoing distributor has **voluntarily discontinued the business**. That is exactly the retiring-seller case.
- The seller surrenders their ARN within 15 days of the transfer.
- 2025 relaxation: a seller who moves to being your sub-distributor is no longer automatically barred, subject to conditions. That makes a **seller transition period** workable.
- Nominees of a deceased MFD now have **12 months** to transfer the AUM, up from 6. These heirs usually have no ARN and no interest in running the business, so they are natural sellers.
- Trail is paid at the **lower** of the seller's and your commission rates. Some summaries mention a cooling-off period. Confirm the current terms with two AMCs before your first deal.

### The economics (illustrative; my assumptions, not market data)

I found **no published deal multiples** for Indian MFD books. The numbers below are assumptions for you to test in the market.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Target book | ₹40 Cr AUM, ~70% equity |
| Blended trail | 0.60-0.65% (equity-heavy books are typically 0.55-0.70%) |
| Annual trail | ≈ ₹25 L |
| Price | 2-2.5× annual trail ≈ ₹50-62 L |
| Structure | 35-40% upfront. The rest is paid over 24-36 months **in proportion to the AUM retained**. |
| Retention after transfer | 80% in year 1 (the base case to underwrite) |
| Year-1 trail after attrition | ≈ ₹20 L |
| Cash yield on full price | ~32-40% a year. Payback in about 3 years, then ongoing income with market growth on top. |

Because most of the price is an earn-out, **the acquired trail largely pays for itself**, and the seller carries the retention risk.

### Three-year target
- **4-6 books** with **₹200-300 Cr** of acquired AUM, which is about **₹1.2-1.9 Cr of annual trail**.
- That income builds the net worth you need to qualify for PMS. Regular PMS needs **₹5 Cr** (at least 10% in unencumbered liquid assets). A PRIM-only manager needs **₹2 Cr**.
- You also get a larger base of ₹25L+ investors who could later move to PRIM.

## 3. How this connects to your PMS plan (new rules)

SEBI notified the **Portfolio Managers Regulations, 2026** on 7 Oct 2026. They take effect on **1 April 2027**.
- **PRIM** (Portfolio Managers Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units): direct-plan MFs, ETFs, index funds and SIFs. The minimum ticket is **₹25 L**, and a PRIM-only manager needs **₹2 Cr** net worth. This is the natural next step from MFD, much closer than a full equity PMS.
- Regular PMS keeps the ₹50 L minimum ticket and the ₹5 Cr net worth requirement.
- **Catch:** SEBI requires PRIM and MF distribution activities and clients to be segregated, except for accredited investors. Plan the structure early, for example a separate company for PRIM, and decide which clients move. Get a SEBI-practice lawyer to sign off.

**Sequence:** grow the MFD book (organically and by buying books) → reach ₹2 Cr net worth in the entity → apply for PRIM in FY28 → move to a full PMS only if the PRIM track record supports it.

## 4. Ninety-day action plan

**Weeks 1-2: set up**
- Write a one-page acquisition thesis: books of ₹15-75 Cr, more than 60% equity, a Mumbai/MMR client base, a seller aged 55+ or a nominee of a deceased MFD, and low client concentration.
- Set your capital limit, for example ₹1 Cr for the first 18 months, and fix the deal template: 35-40% upfront, a retention-linked earn-out, 6-12 months of seller transition and a non-solicit clause.
- Confirm the current AMFI transfer mechanics, rates and timing with 2-3 AMC operations teams.

**Weeks 3-8: build a pipeline of 30 conversations**
- **AMC regional sales managers** are the best source. They know which IFAs are slowing down. Ask 10 RMs for two names each.
- **FIFA and local MFD associations, and Cafemutual events.** Position yourself as "the Mumbai MFD who gives retiring IFAs a clean exit and looks after their clients".
- **Deceased-MFD nominees**, through AMC branches and the R&T agents (CAMS and KFin). Approach them with sensitivity.
- Platform sub-brokers: their AUM often sits under the platform's ARN, so check the transfer path before spending time on them.

**Weeks 6-12: diligence and the first deal**
- Ask for 24 months of CAMS and KFin brokerage statements, AUM by client, the top 20 clients' share of AUM, client age profile, the SIP book, equity vs debt mix, KYC and nominee status, and complaint history.
- Before you sign, the seller makes **joint calls with you to the top 20 clients**. The seller's personal introduction is the main thing that keeps clients from leaving.
- Close **one book**. Track retention every month.

**Stop-loss.** If the first book keeps **less than 70% of AUM after 12 months**, pause buying and fix the integration playbook before the next deal.

## 5. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Clients move to direct plans or another adviser after the transfer | Retention-linked earn-out, seller introductions, a review meeting with every acquired client within 60 days |
| Trail compression (SEBI working group on MFDs, TER changes) | Price on a lower trail rate (underwrite at 0.50%). Keep payback under 3.5 years. |
| A market fall cuts AUM-linked trail by 20-30% | Use earn-outs, not large upfront payments. Keep 6 months of costs in reserve. |
| PRIM segregation rules complicate the MFD-to-PRIM move | Plan the entity structure now, with legal sign-off before April 2027 |
| Acquisitions become the new distraction | Cap it at 2 deals a year and one standard playbook. Say no to anything outside the thesis. |

---

## 6. What to stop or cap so you stay focused
- **Kings content:** shoot and edit in fixed monthly batches, for example 2 days a month. Treat it as a long-term brand asset with no revenue target before year 2. Keep it separate from your financial advice, so nothing reads like investment marketing.
- **Insurance:** sell it only as a service through a broker tie-up. Don't build a book.
- **SME acquisitions from the October shortlist:** park them until book acquisitions are running smoothly and PRIM is filed.
- **Trading and options:** treat it as personal capital, not a business, and keep it out of working hours.
